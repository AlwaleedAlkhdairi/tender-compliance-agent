# NRUC Procurement Evaluation Guidance

> Synthetic dataset created for the SDAIA Academy capstone project.
> Informed by publicly available good-practice principles (e.g. World Bank
> project procurement guidance); all content here is fictional and internal
> to the fictional NRUC.

## 1. Fair and Equal Treatment

All bids must be evaluated against the same published requirements and
weights. Evaluators may not introduce new criteria after bid opening, may
not relax a mandatory requirement for one bidder only, and must document the
evidence behind every judgement so the evaluation can be independently
reviewed.

## 2. Material vs Non-Material Deviations

A deviation is **material** when it affects the scope, quality, delivery
time or price in a way that would give the bidder an unfair advantage or
create unacceptable risk. Material deviations from mandatory requirements
require rejection of the bid. Examples of material deviations:

- A required certification (such as ISO/IEC 27001) that is absent, expired,
  or only "in progress" at the submission deadline.
- A warranty or delivery period that does not meet the stated minimum.
- Absence of the required bid security.

A deviation is **non-material** when it is a historical or administrative
gap that does not change scope, price or risk. Examples: a missing page
number, an unsigned copy where the signed original exists in the package, a
reference contact listed without a phone number. Non-material gaps should be
resolved through clarification, not rejection.

## 3. Clarification Rules

Evaluators may request clarification of what is already in a bid, but a
clarification may never introduce new information that changes the bid's
substance, price or delivery commitments. If mandatory evidence is entirely
absent from the bid, the gap cannot be cured by clarification.

## 4. Evidence Standards

Certificates must be valid at the bid submission deadline and issued by an
accredited body. Reference projects count only when completed (provisional
acceptance reached) and within the stated look-back window. Claims about
performance, staffing or SLAs count as evidence only when the bid states
them specifically (models, numbers, named staff) rather than generically.

## 5. Abnormally Low Bids

A bid materially below the cost estimate or other bids must be analyzed
before financial scoring. The evaluation team must ask whether the price
plausibly covers the required scope, quality and support period. If the
bidder cannot substantiate the price, the bid may be rejected as an
unacceptable performance risk; the analysis and decision must be recorded.

## 6. Conflicts of Interest

Any evaluator with a financial or personal interest in a bidder must be
excluded from the evaluation of that tender. Bidders that participated in
preparing the tender documents are excluded from bidding.

## 7. Recommended Award Decision Record

The evaluation report must contain: the compliance result per bidder with
evidence references, the scored comparison with per-criterion justification,
identified risks and outstanding items per bidder, and a recommendation
naming the best evaluated compliant bid. Missing evidence must be listed
explicitly per bidder so the contracting authority can see what remains
open before contract signature.
